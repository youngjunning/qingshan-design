# Web landing page

Use this reference for product marketing, launch, portfolio, and narrative pages.

## Narrative structure

1. State the product promise and primary action in the first viewport.
2. Show real product evidence or a credible product representation.
3. Explain the workflow or mechanism.
4. Establish proof, differentiation, safety, or trust.
5. Repeat one clear action after the reader has enough context.

Do not turn the page into a sequence of interchangeable rounded feature cards.

## Qingshan Design application

- Let mist and lake atmosphere establish identity in the hero, then reduce decoration as information density rises.
- Use scenic layers as gradients, restrained illustration, or low-density particles; do not use a literal tourism photo as the entire UI.
- Keep hero copy on an opaque or controlled-contrast region. The atmosphere may surround the message but must not lower its legibility.
- Use wildlife, leaves, or water movement as one signature motif, not a collection of simultaneous effects.
- Keep primary actions lake-teal strong, secondary actions quiet, and warm dawn accents rare.

## Motion and performance

- Make the first meaningful content readable before decorative code or media finishes.
- Use responsive images and explicit dimensions. Do not send desktop-sized hero art to narrow phones.
- Avoid preloading noncritical decoration. Prioritize the actual LCP image or text region.
- Pause Canvas and animation when offscreen or when the page is hidden.
- Under Reduced Motion, remove travel, parallax, particle wakes, and looping wildlife; preserve a static composition.

## Responsive composition

- **Desktop:** split hero is acceptable when copy and product evidence both remain strong.
- **Tablet:** reduce overlap and decorative depth; keep proof close to the promise.
- **Phone:** stack copy, action, and product evidence; keep primary action reachable and avoid clipped headline rotators.
- Verify long localized headings and 200% zoom before treating the hero as complete.
